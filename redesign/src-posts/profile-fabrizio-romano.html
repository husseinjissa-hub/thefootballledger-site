The individual who became football's most powerful newsroom.

Fabrizio Romano spent seven years on the streets of Milan earning the trust of agents, directors and players. Then he took the stories off the broadcast schedule and onto his own channels, at any hour, in every market. "Here We Go" turned a contact book into a brand. Today his Instagram and X following is larger than Sky Sports, ESPN FC and BBC Sport combined.

The genesis

As an adolescent growing up in a football mad city like Naples, Fabrizio Romano dreamt of a future as a football journalist. In a way his story reflects that of the most successful players in the game; as they realize their goals they find themselves acquiring a reality very different to that in which they dreamt of. In recent years Fabrizio has become one of the most prominent figures in football culture. You can find him in EA Sports FC (one of the biggest video games in the world), attracting crowds and signing memorabilia in public, and attracting more followers, likes, and views across a variety of social media platforms than many of the biggest sports media outlets combined. His astronomical growth is a reflection of cultural shifts in media and football but his roots were planted in traditional journalism.

After a few months of working for a small local football outlet in his native Italy, he got a phone call from an aspiring agent in Barcelona. This agent asked him to write a piece about two young players, Mauro Icardi and Gerard Deulofeu, from La Masia (Barcelona's famed Academy). Fabrizio kept in contact with this agent and as a result of this relationship Fabrizio was given the nod to break a story when a young Mauro Icardi was preparing for his move to Sampdoria from Barcelona in 2011. Two years later Mauro Icardi had become one of the most renowned strikers in Italy's top flight and was a highly sought after young talent. As a result of the spontaneous relationship made two years prior, Fabrizio had gotten the inside scoop from Icardi's agent in winter of 2013 that the player was going to be sold to Internazionale Milano from Sampdoria during the upcoming summer transfer window for a reported €13 million. Fabrizio ran with the story and it exploded across the Italian media, very few believed it to be true, but in July of 2013 Fabrizio's months old rumour was proven as gospel and shortly after he landed a life changing role with Sky Sports Italia.

Fabrizio admits that he was bitten with the transfer bug in his adolescence, similarly to most football fanatics, but breaking his first impactful story had turned this bug into a full on obsession. "The obsession is the crucial point" says Fabrizio, he was now indebted to the rush of breaking news to the footballing world.

Gaining trust

As a young and still relatively unproven journalist, Fabrizio found that punching in and punching out of the Sky Italia office wasn't doing it for him. So he opted to spend his days prowling the streets of Milan, a hub of European Football business, for stories instead of waiting for them to arrive at his desk. He claims to have spent about 10-12 hours every single day over the next 7 years in the streets of Milan and he always carried the same objective in mind…to build relationships.

The Footballing industry is a vast and sprawling web, a sticky one at that, and Fabrizio understood the importance of building a healthy reputation for himself within this web. He would wait outside popular hotels, cafes, and bars for hours upon hours to ensure that whenever someone from the football web (agents, directors, players) stepped out that they were met by him and his probing questions. He admits that at first it often proved difficult to get any information of substance from these figures but over time he believed that:

"Being there every single day and [being] respectful and if you show them that you don't want just the big headline, you want to build a relationship, you can help them… still working for a big media like Sky… all these factors will bring [him] to the final result." — Fabrizio Romano, in interview

Following this formula with discipline and diligence compounded over years has in fact brought him to the "final result". Fabrizio is one of, if not the, most well connected individuals in European Football. He always took it one step beyond just a functioning business relationship, he is deeply trusted by a vast majority of agents, teams, players, directors, and presidents from all over Europe. He has shared stories about knowing a player is going to get transferred before the player himself is even aware of it, proving his deep relationships between agents and club directors. Vice versa, he has shared stories where he knows a player has already agreed to join a new club before his old club director is aware of it, proving his relationship with some players and their agents.

By the turn of this decade his reputation had become deeply rooted in the footballing web but his name was still largely anonymous outside of it…

Granting access

Fabrizio had reached the summit of football journalism within the traditional media. But this wasn't the reason why he became a football journalist in the first place. He did so because he had an "obsession" for breaking news from all aspects of the game, especially transfers, to the world before anyone else. In 2020 Fabrizio was live on TV for Sky Italia. When he had gotten off air he checked his phone and saw dozens of missed phone calls and messages regarding a big transfer story which, by the time he got to his phone, had already been made public by other outlets. This moment served to be a catalyst for Fabrizio's name to infiltrate footballing culture and separate himself from traditional media.

He felt as though traditional media had taken him as far as it could in terms of helping him fulfill his obsession for breaking general football news and transfer stories. The world was evolving especially in regards to media and the distribution of information. Nowadays, people around the world demand stimulating news and content at all times and consume it at an unprecedented rate. Fabrizio understood that restrictions around traditional media in terms of its distribution and access meant that it was slowly being left behind by the masses, especially the younger generation. He was in a very unique position to capitalize on this evolving space.

By combining his attributes such as: trust, a vast network of connections, accuracy, and consistency, he acquired throughout his time working for a massive traditional media outlet like Sky Sports with the unparalleled reach and immediacy of modern media outlets such as Instagram, X (Twitter), YouTube, TikTok, and Twitch, Fabrizio had almost instantaneously become one of the most prominent figures in the football world.

Embracing social media also removed the geographical and temporal barriers that had traditionally restricted the reach of football journalists. A journalist working within a traditional newsroom could report to a national or regional audience, but Fabrizio could distribute a transfer story instantly to millions of followers across every major football market in the world. More importantly, he was no longer bound by broadcasting schedules, print deadlines, or the operating hours of a newsroom. Instagram, X, YouTube, TikTok and Twitch allowed him to publish at any hour of the day, 365 days a year, creating a level of immediacy that traditional media could rarely match. This is only going to become increasingly important as younger generations continue consuming news primarily through social media, where information is expected to be immediate, constant, and easily accessible. As this appetite for instant stimulation continues to grow, Fabrizio's ability to distribute information within this ecosystem has become just as important to his success as the information he receives.

"Here. We. Go."

Fabrizio isn't the only journalist to have broken away from the mold of traditional media and gone on to build a strong following from delivering news on all things football. David Ornstein, Gianluca Di Marzio, and Florian Plettenberg to name just a few are other prevalent sources of football reporting you can find operating on social media but none of them stand anywhere close to Fabrizio in comparison.

So what set him apart from the rest and led him to his celebrity status?

Branding and cultural relevance, in that order. In 2020 he stumbled across his iconic catch phrase "Here. We. Go" during Bruno Fernandes' drawn out transfer saga involving Manchester United and his former club Sporting Lisbon. Fabrizio had been reporting on the transfer for weeks and when it had finally gone through he captioned a post confirming the transfer of a player with "Here We Go" for the first time. He couldn't have anticipated the impact that word selection would have on the rest of his life. Before he knew it fans from other clubs were asking for "Here We Go"s relating to their clubs transfer business. Naturally, he turned it into his way of signing off on a breaking story almost how an artist would sign their name on the bottom of a painting. Just like that, his name/reputation within the football web now had a brand attached to it which allowed him to permeate football culture and eventually mainstream pop culture. "Here We Go" is now synonymous around the world with a completed transfer. Clubs will post "Here We Go"s now on their own social media accounts to announce a new signing, some clubs have even collaborated with Fabrizio on posts while announcing their new signings.

In many ways football is a reflection of culture. In regards to global culture, we are living in a period where a lot of human attention is allocated to our screens. Which means the modern football fan wants to consume football related content 365 days a year, something Fabrizio is well placed to do. The traditional football calendar operates over roughly 10 months of the year, luckily for Fabrizio, these 2 months of rest in the summer include the summer transfer window. There's never a shortage of transfer stories (supply) in the summer and there's certainly no shortage of consumers (demand) because football fans have much more screen time to fill than they normally would during the season. This period over the last few years has proved crucial in Fabrizio's content rising to the top of many algorithms across the world, thus further weaving his brand into football culture. Not to mention him and his catch phrase "Here We Go" have been added to EA Sports FC (formerly FIFA) which is one of, if not the most, played and watched video games of the 2000's.

The scale of Fabrizio's digital and cultural presence is quite startling when placed alongside the social media followings of the traditional media institutions which not that long ago dominated global news. As the graphic demonstrates, Fabrizio's combined following across Instagram and X dwarfs that of Sky Sports, ESPN FC, BBC Sport and the other major reporting outlets represented. More importantly, the comparison illustrates a fundamental shift in where authority can be built. The largest audience in this sample does not belong to a newsroom, broadcaster or established media corporation, but to one journalist operating under his own name, from wherever he wants, whenever he wants, across the world. Fabrizio's cultural relevance has allowed his brand to compete with—and in social reach, surpass—institutions with vastly greater traditional media infrastructure.

Where it goes from here

Fabrizio didn't just create his personal newsroom, he may actually be trying to reinvent what a newsroom is. He capitalized on global culture and football's shift from newspapers and broadcasters towards immediate and personalized distribution, thus, unlocking a level of reach and influence he would've never imagined possible. But recently he has suggested that he may not continue in the transfer industry "as [he's] doing right now" which raises a bigger question: what comes next? Having already transformed how football news reaches its audience, Fabrizio's next revolution could be transforming what that audience expects from a football newsroom entirely — moving beyond simply breaking the news to owning the conversation around it. His envisioned change may be the beginning of this next chapter in football media, or, he could have his eyes set on something completely unexpected.
